PRP, Shockwave, and Steroid Injections: What CCC's Data Means for Future Medical Costs in Auto Injury Claims

By Chris Pittaluga, RN, JD, CLCP | Invictus Life Care Planning

In my last post, I looked at Gen Re's finding that long-term care, not one-time damages, drives the largest bodily injury claims. This week's news comes from the other end of the severity spectrum: everyday auto injury claims. The cost driver there is a fast-growing group of outpatient procedures.

CCC Intelligent Solutions, whose data is widely used in auto claims, published The Rise of Low-Complexity Surgical Interventions in Auto Casualty BI Claims by Erik Bahnsen, Director of Industry Analytics. Its central point is that auto bodily injury (BI) costs are now being pushed up less by fusions and discectomies and more by low-complexity procedures used often and early: corticosteroid injections, platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy, and extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT). The trend drew more attention on October 15, 2026, when PropertyCasualty360 reported that medical costs and litigation are driving up auto claim severity.

The Numbers

Citing ISS Fast Track data, CCC reports that:

  • The average paid per personal auto BI claim is rising 9 to 10% year over year.
  • It has risen more than 30% over the past four years and now exceeds $30,000 per feature.
  • 70% of that inflation has occurred since Q1 2020.

CCC's medical bill review data adds detail:

  • PRP treatments average $11,000 to $12,000 per injured party, usually across three to four injections, and are increasingly given in ambulatory surgery centers instead of office settings. CCC notes that PRP is generally not reimbursed by Medicare, Medicaid, or private insurance for musculoskeletal soft tissue injuries.
  • Steroid injections average three per claimant, often scheduled as a "series of three."
  • Shockwave therapy averages eight to nine sessions per claimant and is spreading across jurisdictions.
  • Procedures happen sooner. Experimental procedures now average 122 days from the date of loss, down from 166 days three years ago.
  • About 40% of low-complexity surgical treatments are in claimants age 40 and under.

Why Future Medical Claims Matter Most

CCC states that injection-based therapies are "increasingly tied to future medical demands." A claim for indefinite repeat injections can be worth far more than the treatment already billed. CCC also notes that recurring injection schedules don't often match how group health plans handle the same care.

For a younger claimant, projecting a series of PRP or steroid injections over decades can add a large amount to a demand. Whether that projection holds up depends on the foundation behind it.

The Life Care Planning Angle

A credible life care plan doesn't assume that every procedure a claimant has received will repeat for life. It asks:

  1. Is it medically necessary? Is the treatment supported by objective findings, such as exam and imaging, and not by reported pain alone?
  2. What does the evidence show? CCC notes that some experts see a lack of high-level evidence for PRP in neck, back, and joint sprain/strain injuries. It also notes that experts describe corticosteroid injections as mostly palliative, not curative.
  3. Is there documented response? Repeat treatment should follow a documented reevaluation showing benefit, not a pre-set schedule.
  4. How long and how often? The plan should state frequency, duration, and the expected course of the injury, including the natural healing of soft tissue injuries.
  5. What does it really cost? Costs should reflect the right setting (office or surgery center) and current, sourced pricing.

This works for both sides. Plaintiff counsel needs future medical projections that can survive a challenge. Defense counsel and adjusters need a clear way to separate supported future care from speculative care. A carefully sourced, individualized life care plan does both.

CCC's data describes auto bodily injury costs being pushed up by low-complexity procedures used often and early, and future medical demands that can be worth far more than the treatment already billed. Projecting that care depends on medical necessity, the evidence, a documented response, how long and how often treatment would continue, and what it costs in the right setting.

Sources: Erik Bahnsen, "The Rise of Low-Complexity Surgical Interventions in Auto Casualty BI Claims," CCC Intelligent Solutions, 2026; Maura Keller, "Medical costs, litigation drive up auto claim severity," PropertyCasualty360, October 15, 2026. Figures are as reported by CCC.
